An editor already knows what a keyword is. It knows a quote from a comment, and it knows the plate on top of the stack is the one that matters. Syntect, the library, is the Rust that reads those themes. Tristan Hume wrote it. It is MIT. `bat` uses it. Helix uses it. That project is not this ticker.

SYNTECT is a desk built next to that idea. The pair beside it is MSFT. The pair is a name on a launch, not the share, and not Microsoft. Pons did not write the library either. Nothing on the site is a solicitation, and nothing on it is yield.

## Two names

Put the two plates on the bench and leave a hair of light between them. One plate is the ink. The other is the pair it sits beside. People will try to read the second plate as the company. It is not. Say that in the first line, every time, or the rest of the room is a misunderstanding.

The fee on that pair is one percent. It does not spray itself into wallets. Pons credits it to an escrow. A press, deployed before the launch and named as the fee recipient, pulls what has been credited. A quarter of what it pulls stays in a gift pot. The rest is a desk reserve, taken by hand by the owner. After the bond, if the fee arrives as the pair token instead of ETH, that token is held. It is not spent by a script.

## What a holder actually does

Come in without a wallet and the rooms still move. Ink, Dive, and Hunt are on the table. Ink hands you four brushes and a page: magenta for the word the grammar claims, gold for anything in quotes, green for the aside, bone for a bare name. Punctuation is already dry. A clean page scores 100. That number lives in the browser that made it. It is not a balance.

Dive is a stack. Similar names sit underneath on purpose. The only true plate is the one on top. Close that one. Closing a lower plate is how you miss.

Hunt is a grid. The comment is green and quiet. The trap is a gold quote with a slash in it. A slash inside quotes is still a string. Tap the comment. Leave the quote.

Detect is the beam. Paste a file, or pick one already on the desk, and a small scanner paints it. Keyword, string, comment, number, function. It is a toy, so the brushes have something to read. It is not the Rust library. The library remains on crates.io, under Hume’s name, with his license.

Connect a wallet and Room opens. The same three games take that address as their plate for the day, so two people do not play the same page. The score still does not leave the browser.

## The gift

A gift is a single claim. It opens only when four things are true at once: the press has been switched on with the token address, the wallet holds the token, the pot can cover the size the owner set, and fifteen minutes have passed since that wallet last claimed. Then the button is there. Press it, sign once, and a fixed amount of ETH leaves the pot. It is not interest. It does not compound. If the pot is short, the button stays dark. If the token is gone from the wallet, it stays dark. The owner cannot tip the pot out the back. The owner takes a gift the same way anyone else does: by holding, and by waiting.

## What the pictures are for

The avatar is a beam and three dots. Lime, then magenta, gold, sage. It has no letters, so it still reads when it is small. The banner is the bench: ink stone, brush, comment slip, blue plate, one scan line. The 5:2 cover is seven plates, one for each scope, the last of them in the beam. Use that on the article. The article picture is the table itself, shot low, with the jars in the foreground.

None of these are a chart. None of them are a promise. The room is a darkroom. You paint, you close the top plate, you leave the trap alone, and if a gift is actually there, you take it once.
